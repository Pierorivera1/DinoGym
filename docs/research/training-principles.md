## Findings

### 1. Rep ranges

- About three-quarters of training volume should fall in **6–15 reps**, and sets above 15 or below 6 should be used sparingly [F3].
- Match the progression method to the range: add reps in moderate-to-high ranges, add load in low-to-moderate ranges [PMG].
- No free source gives a fixed split like "compound 6–10, isolation 10–15". That split is our own choice within his 6–15 guidance.

### 2. Proximity to failure

- Most working sets should stop with **1–2 reps in reserve** (RIR). Beginners can use 1–3 RIR [BLOG].
- Take the **last set of each low-risk exercise to failure** [BLOG], [PMG].
- Keep 1–2 RIR even on the last set of heavy squats, bench presses and deadlifts [PMG].
- The research he cites:
  - Probably Refalo et al. 2024, *J Sports Sci*: going to failure and stopping at 1–2 RIR gave similar growth in trained lifters. The author name is inferred from the DOI.
  - Robinson et al.'s meta-regressions: more growth the closer sets end to failure.

### 3. Rest between sets

- **Compound exercises:** 3–5 min in 2018 [F5]. About 2–3 min on the Ultimate PPL page [UPPL].
- **Isolation exercises:** 1–2 min [F5].
- Never rest less than 1 min [PMG].
- **To save time:** use the low end of each range and superset non-competing exercises [UPPL], [ESS].
- The research he cites: Schoenfeld et al. 2016, *JSCR*, where 3 min rest beat 1 min.
- No guidance was found on rest between exercises.

### 4. Volume and frequency

- **Weekly sets:** 10–20 hard sets per muscle per week. About 6 sets can work when they are taken to failure [HALF].
- **Frequency:** train each muscle about **2× a week**; 3× is not shown to be better [BRO].
- **Sets per exercise:** 2–3 for compound exercises and 1–2 for isolation [ESS]; 3–4 at home [HOME].

### 5. Progression and deloads

- **Double progression** [PMG]. Add reps at the same load until you reach the top of the range, then add load and return to the bottom. His example: 3×10 builds to 13, then add load and restart at 10.
- **"2-for-2" rule** [F1]. If you beat the target by 2 reps on the last set in two sessions in a row, add load.
- **Deloads** [DEL]:
  - Every 4–8 weeks.
  - Cut volume by 30–50% and keep effort at RPE 6–8.
  - Beginners may not need them.

### 6. Splits

- **Tier ranking** [SPLIT]:
  - **S:** the 4-day Upper/Lower split (his current favourite), UL/PPL, and full body.
  - **A:** PPL.
  - **B:** 6-day Upper/Lower.
  - **C:** bro split.
- **The Essentials program, built for busy people** [ESS]:
  - 4× a week: U, L, Rest, U, L, Rest, Rest.
  - 5× a week: U, L, Rest, Push, Pull, Legs, Rest.
  - The page says the split is flexible.
- **The Ultimate PPL program, 5× a week** [UPPL]: Push, Pull, Legs, Rest, Upper, Lower, Rest.
- **Not found:** a video in which he says U/L/Rest/U/L is optimal for people with less time. [ESS] and [SPLIT] are the closest evidence.

### 7. Bodyweight training

- The rep count matters less than getting "sufficiently close to failure" [HOME].
- His home plan [HOME]:
  - Full body 3–5× a week.
  - 1–2 exercises per muscle, 3–4 sets each.
  - 1–3 RIR, with the last set to failure.
- Ways to progress without adding load: harder leverage, deficits, unilateral work, added load, constant tension, and pauses [HOME].
- [S17] supports "load matters little when sets end close to failure". It is research consensus, not something Nippard cites.
